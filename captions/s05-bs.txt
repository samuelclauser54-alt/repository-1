The Black–Scholes equation comes from one trick: an option and its stock can cancel each other's randomness.

Itô's lemma on the option value V(S, t) gives a random part of Δ·σS·dW, where Δ = ∂V/∂S. Hold the option and short Δ shares, and the stock's random part, also Δ·σS·dW, cancels it.

I simulated it (1-yr at-the-money call, σ = 20%, 10,000 paths). The seller's profit spread:
• do nothing: $18.98
• hedge monthly: $1.86
• weekly: $0.93
• daily: $0.43

A riskless portfolio has to earn the risk-free rate (or there's free money), and setting dΠ = rΠ dt gives
∂V/∂t + ½σ²S² ∂²V/∂S² + rS ∂V/∂S − rV = 0

μ, the stock's expected return, cancels out completely, which is why episode 1's price didn't depend on it.
