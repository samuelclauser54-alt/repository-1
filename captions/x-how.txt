How 100 AIs taught themselves to fly, in 30 seconds. 🔊 Sound on.

This is what was happening in the gates video that 1.2 million of you watched:

1. Each flyer is a tiny neural network. It sees 4 numbers: how far the next gap is above or below it, its own vertical speed, the distance to the gate, and where the gap after that is.
2. It turns those into 1 decision, 30 times a second: flap, or don't.
3. After every round, the 30 that flew furthest are kept. The other 270 are deleted and replaced with copies of the winners, each with small random changes.
4. Repeat. By generation 40, 80 of the best 100 clear all 14 gates on a course they have never seen.

Nobody writes the rules for how to fly, and the networks never see an example. The only feedback is one number: how far it got. This is called neuroevolution.

Everything on screen is from the real run, not an illustration. The dots in the grid are generation 8, ranked by how long each one lasted.

You can train your own in the browser and change the rules: samuelclauser54-alt.github.io/evolve-lab
