This fire is 46 lines of Python. 🔥 The code on screen is the code drawing it.

There is no physics in it. The whole simulation is one rule, run 30 times a second:
• every cell copies the heat of a cell in the row below it
• usually the one straight below, sometimes one step to the left or right, at random
• and loses a little heat on the way up

The bottom row is the fuel: a few hot spots that sway and flicker. Heat that climbs far enough cools to nothing, which is what makes the flame tips. Halfway through I add wind, which just shifts where each cell copies from.

Each cell's heat then picks two things: a character (denser = hotter) and a colour from black through red, orange and yellow to white.

It is a version of the fire effect from the PlayStation port of DOOM (1995). It only needs NumPy and runs in a normal terminal:
