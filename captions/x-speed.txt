Part 4: the course is 83% faster. 100 AIs learning to fly through gates, starting from nothing. 🕹️

Same flyers, same gravity, same flap. The only change is how fast the gates come at them: 5.5 units a second, up from 3. That leaves about 0.7 seconds between gates to get lined up.
• Generation 1: 24 of 100 get through the first gate, and 2 are left after the second
• Generation 5: 16 make it through the whole 20-second course
• Generation 25: 62
• Generation 85: 98

The 15 gates you see in the last run take them 12 seconds. All 98 that finish those also finish the full 26-gate course.

Each AI is a tiny neural network (6 inputs, 8 neurons, 1 output: flap or don't). Every generation, 300 networks fly 3 random courses and get one number back: how far they got. The top 30 survive and are copied with small random changes. The 100 you see are that generation's best, on a course they were not trained on.

Odd result: speed turned out to be easier for them than moving gates (Part 2) or flipping gravity (Part 3).
