What a chord looks like 🔊

One note goes to the left speaker and the other to the right. Plot left against right and you get these Lissajous figures, which is exactly what an oscilloscope shows.

• octave 2:1, fifth 3:2, fourth 4:3, major third 5:4: simple whole-number ratios, so the pattern closes into a loop and the chord sounds smooth
• tritone ≈ √2:1: there's no simple ratio, so the curve never repeats. It fills the square, and the interval sounds tense

Pythagoras noticed the ratio part 2,500 years ago with strings. (Pure ratios here. A piano's equal tuning is slightly off from them, which is why its figures slowly spin.)
