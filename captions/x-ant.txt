One ant, two rules, and 10,000 steps of chaos. Then, out of nowhere, it starts building a road. 🐜

The rules (Langton's ant, 1986):
• on a light square, turn right; on a dark square, turn left
• flip the square's color and step forward
That's the whole program.

For about 10,000 steps it makes an unpredictable mess. Then at step 9,976 it falls into a 104-step cycle that repeats forever, and each lap moves it 2 squares diagonally: the "highway." Turn the sound on and you can hear it lock into the rhythm.

The strange part: it seems to build a highway no matter what pattern you start it on, but nobody has ever proved that it always does. Two rules, and the question is still open.
