Two chemicals, the same two equations. Change two numbers and leopard spots turn into zebra stripes. 🐆🦓

Chemical V turns U into more V wherever they meet (U + 2V → 3V). U is constantly fed in and V is constantly removed, and both spread out, U twice as fast. That's it. Top: feed 0.037, kill 0.065. Bottom: feed 0.029, kill 0.057. Both start from 6 tiny seeds.

The spots even split in two as they grow, like cells dividing.

In 1952, Alan Turing (yes, the codebreaker) proposed that chemicals reacting and spreading like this could create the patterns on animals. It's now a leading explanation for some real ones: zebrafish stripes and the ridges in the roof of your mouth have been linked to Turing-style systems.

The model here is Gray–Scott reaction–diffusion, about 24,000 steps on a 300×260 grid, simulated in NumPy.
