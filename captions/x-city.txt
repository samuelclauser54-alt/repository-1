Nobody in this city minds being outnumbered 2 to 1. Watch what happens anyway. 🏙️

A 50 × 50 city: 45% blue households, 45% orange, 10% empty lots, mixed at random. One rule: a household moves only if fewer than 1 in 3 of its neighbors share its color. Everyone is fine living as a minority.
• At the start, the average household has 49% matching neighbors, and 19% want to move
• After 1,021 moves, nobody wants to move
• The average household now has 78% matching neighbors, and 34% have no neighbor of the other color at all

Nobody asked for that. Each move slightly tips the neighbors left behind, some of them move too, and the mild preferences add up to a divided map.

This is Thomas Schelling's model from 1971, part of the work behind his Nobel Prize in economics. It's a toy model with one rule, not a description of any real city. Real segregation has many other causes, including laws and money. The point is narrower: a pattern can be far more extreme than what any single person in it wants.
