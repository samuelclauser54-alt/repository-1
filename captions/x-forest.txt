100 AIs learning to fly through a forest. 🌲

Every flyer moves forward on its own; the only control is the engine (hold to climb, let go to fall). A pine tree grows from the ground about every 5 units, each 45 to 75% as tall as the gap under the canopy. Touch a tree, the ground or the canopy and you're out.
• Generation 1: 1 of 100
• Generation 3: 21 of 100
• Generation 22: 65 of 100
• Generation 90: 75 of 100

Each AI is a tiny neural network (9 inputs, 8 neurons, 1 output: engine power). It feels how far the canopy and the ground (trees included) are right where it is and 1.5, 3 and 4.5 units ahead, plus its own vertical speed. Nobody told it what a tree is. Every generation, 300 networks fly 3 random forests and get one score: how far they got. The top 30 survive and are copied with small random changes. The 100 you see are that generation's best, in a forest they never trained in.

Most of them learned to skim just under the canopy, above the treetops. The best generation gets 75 of 100 through.
