One driver taps the brakes for one second, and 30 seconds later there's a traffic jam. 🚗

22 cars on a ring road. No crash, no merge, nothing in the road. One car brakes briefly at the start. Each driver just reacts to the gap in front, a little too late, and the slowdown grows every time it's passed back until cars come to a full stop. Then the jam sits there and drifts backwards, against the traffic, long after the first driver is gone.

The inner ring plots each car's speed, so you can watch the dip slide backwards.

This isn't just a model. In 2008, Sugiyama's team in Japan put 22 real cars on a 230 m circle and told them to drive at a steady 30 km/h. A stop-and-go jam appeared out of nowhere, just like this. (The simulation is the "optimal velocity" car-following model.)

The fix: leave a bigger gap and brake gently. You absorb the wave instead of passing it on.
