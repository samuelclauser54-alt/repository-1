Rocket series, part 7: thrown sideways. 100 AIs learning to land. 🚀

Same rocket, pad and gravity. Every rocket starts with an extra 5 units per second of sideways speed, to the left or the right at random. To land it has to lean against the motion, kill the sideways speed, come back over the pad and straighten up before touching down.
• Generation 1: 0 of 100 land
• Generation 30: 1 of 100 land
• Generation 40: 34 of 100 land
• Generation 120: 89 of 100 land

This is the slowest start of the series: after 30 generations only 1 of 100 lands. Then it takes off: 34 by generation 40, 70 by generation 60. The trained rockets lean about 50% further than in part 1 to cancel the sideways speed.

Each AI is a tiny neural network (7 inputs, 12 neurons, 2 outputs: engine and steering). Every generation, 300 networks fly 4 test flights and get one score (closer to the pad, slower and more upright is better). The top 30 survive and are copied with small random changes. The 100 you see are that generation's best. A landing only counts if it is soft, upright and on the pad.
