100 AIs learning to stay in orbit, starting from nothing. 🛰️

Every ship starts 4 to 8 units from the planet, moving at only 30 to 85% of the speed an orbit needs there. Left alone it falls in. It has an engine and a small fuel budget, and when the fuel is gone it coasts. Too slow and it hits the planet; too fast and it flies past the red line and is lost.
• Generation 1: 4 of 100 are still flying after 20 seconds
• Generation 3: 33
• Generation 8: 67
• Generation 25: 94

The orbits hold: I ran the final generation for a full minute, long after the fuel was gone, and the same 94 were still circling.

Each AI is a tiny neural network (4 inputs, 8 neurons, 2 outputs). It sees its distance from the planet, its speed toward or away from it, its sideways speed and its fuel. Gravity is the real inverse-square law. Every generation, 300 networks fly 3 sets of random starts and get one number back: how long they lasted. The top 30 survive and are copied with small random changes. The 100 you see are that generation's best.

Nobody told them what an orbit is. The only feedback was how long each one lasted.
