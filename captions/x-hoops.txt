100 AIs learning to shoot a basketball. All each one knows is how far away the hoop is. 🏀

Each AI is a tiny neural network (1 input, 6 neurons, 2 outputs: launch angle and launch speed). Nobody tells it about gravity or arcs. A shot only counts if it drops through the rim clean: no backboard, and touching the rim is a miss.
• Generation 1: 2 of 100 score
• Generation 8: 21
• Generation 40: 49
• Generation 110: all 100, from 1.5 m out to 8 m (past the three-point line)

How it learns: every generation, 300 networks each take 12 shots from random distances and get a score back (makes, then how close the misses were). The top 30 survive and are copied with small random changes. The changes get smaller every generation, which is what lets the late generations fine-tune.

What's real and what isn't: the rim height (3.05 m), rim width, ball size and gravity are real. There is no air resistance and no spin, and a real shooter has to control muscles, not just pick two numbers. So this is the physics problem solved, not the sport.
