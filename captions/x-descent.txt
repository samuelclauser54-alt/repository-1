I dropped 1,000 AIs on a landscape and told them to find the lowest point. Each one only knows which way is downhill. 🏔️

Round 1, just walk downhill: 21% reach the deepest valley (the gold ring). Everyone else gets stuck in the nearest dip and stays there forever, because from inside a valley every direction is uphill.

Round 2, same 1,000 starting spots, but every step also gets a random shake that fades to zero: 66% reach the deepest valley. Early on the shaking knocks them out of shallow dips. By the time it stops, most have settled in the deep one.

This is how AI models train: the "landscape" is the error, and gradient descent walks downhill on it. Real models have billions of dimensions, not 2, but the stuck-in-a-dip problem is real. Randomness (noisy mini-batch gradients, annealing) is part of the fix. Here it's simulated annealing, simulated in NumPy.
